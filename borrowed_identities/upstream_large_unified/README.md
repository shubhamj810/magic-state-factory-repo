# Magic State Factory Search

Code and data for:

> S. Singh, C. Gidney, C. Jones, **"Borrowed Identities: Malleability and Unified Search of Distillation
> Circuits Across the Clifford Hierarchy,"** [arXiv:2606.28518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28518) (2026).

We introduce a *borrowed-identity* condition for magic-state distillation factories: a circuit
need only act as the identity on one input state, not the full codespace. The condition holds at
every level of the Clifford hierarchy and turns factory-finding into a search problem. This
repository implements the search, classifies what it finds, and catalogues the results. See the
paper for the theory.

## Repository structure

| folder | contents |
|---|---|
| [`searches/`](searches/) | The unified search (`unified_search.py`, SAT-free with an optional last-resort SAT stage), the methods it combines (two-group, shared-check gluing), the sequential/malleable construction, the minimality provers, and the scripts that draw the paper's figures. **[README](searches/README.md)** |
| [`classification/`](classification/) | Turns a factory's gate list into `(degree, essential_dim, t_count, decomposition)`, and reconstructs a factory's explicit circuit or a Quirk link from a catalogue row. **[README](classification/README.md)** |
| [`outputs/`](outputs/) | `smallest_per_class.csv` (smallest factory per output class) and `all_factories.csv` (every distinct factory; its Quirk links are in `all_factories_with_quirk.csv.zip`), both with search stage, regenerated by `unified_search.py` for any range; the source of the paper's Tables SII(a-c); the per-level catalogues and raw sweep logs. **[README](outputs/README.md)** |
| [`figures/`](figures/) | Generated plots and Quirk circuit diagrams. **[README](figures/README.md)** |

[`pipeline.ipynb`](pipeline.ipynb), at the root, runs the whole chain: search, classify,
reconstruct a circuit (matrix or Quirk link), re-verify it, or look up a factory already in the
catalogue.

## Main findings

The Clifford-hierarchy level `l` fixes which magic state a factory consumes: `θ=π/2^l`, with
`l=3` the level `T` lives at (full hierarchy table in `searches/README.md`). Numbers below are
combined across `l=2,3,4`.

- **One search covers distance 2.** `searches/unified_search.py` combines three SAT-free stages,
  two-group circuits, output blocks sharing one check register (the synthillation family) and
  CNOT relabeling of the output block, plus an optional fourth SAT stage (`--sat`) used only as a
  last resort. At `l<=3` no sign search is needed: a sign flip is a Clifford correction on the checks. Over `l∈{2,3,4}`, `k≤6`, circuits of `N≤11` qubits and up to 4 check
  qubits it finds **591 distinct verified factories in 86 output classes**, plus 973
  Clifford-padded variants (CNOT relabelings that add output wires; they add no output class and are counted
  separately). Both lists are in `outputs/`: `smallest_per_class.csv` (with Quirk links) and
  `all_factories.csv` (all 1,564 rows; the `search_stage` column separates the 591 from the padded variants;
  Quirk links in `all_factories_with_quirk.csv.zip`; see `outputs/README.md`). Each row has its Clifford-reduced decomposition into `T`/`CS`/`CCZ`-type
  generators, an exact `Z(π/2^l)` count, and the **search stage** that found it (`two-group`, `gluing`,
  `padding`, `SAT`). The two-group stage runs in seconds; the full pipeline, including the optional
  classification of every output, takes about 30 minutes on a laptop (about 10 of them the search itself).
- **Regenerate for any range.** `python3 searches/unified_search.py [--levels ... --kmax ... --nmax ...
  --rmax ... --relabel ... --sat] [--out-dir DIR]` writes both CSVs (with Quirk links and search stage)
  and `search_timing.json` (wall-clock per stage) for whatever parameter range you choose.
- **The two-group stage alone reproduces the paper's sweep**: 21,920 valid (parameter-tuple, sign)
  configurations for `l∈{2,3,4}`, `k≤7`, `N≤11` circuit qubits, `s_total,s_O≤7`; in the unified search
  the two-group stage gives 534 distinct factories directly, and 902 more after Clifford padding.
- **Mixed-output factories beat distilling their parts separately.** At `l=3`, from `T` inputs,
  one factory delivers `CCZ123 CS45` in `n=18` inputs (`[[18,5,2]]`) instead of `8+12=20`, `CS12 T3` in
  `n=20` instead of `26`, and `CCZ123 CS45 T6` in `n=26` instead of `32`; all 11 mixed targets with
  `k<=6` save inputs. `searches/synthillation_compare.py` checks every target against synthillation.
- **Exact minimality.** `searches/coset_certify.py` proves the smallest `n` for a target on a fixed
  number `N` of circuit qubits (exact up to `N=7`); 12 targets are proved smallest.
- **Three factories need the SAT stage at `l=3`**: `[[16,4,2]]`, `[[16,5,2]]` and `[[12,6,2]]` have
  outputs no block partition expresses even after CNOT relabeling. The optional SAT stage finds all
  three (2 s, 60 s, 7 s) and they are in the catalogue. Details in `outputs/README.md`.
- **The sequential search finds no new factories.** It reproduces the paper's malleable chain
  `[[8,3,2]]→[[12,2,2]]→[[14,1,2]]`, and every factory it finds is also found by the unified
  search at equal or smaller `N`. Its role is the construction behind the paper's
  malleable circuits, one parent circuit with many distillation targets, not a source of new
  records. Details in `searches/README.md`.

## An active, extensible tool

This repository is meant to keep being used, not just to reproduce the paper's figures. The
borrowed-identity condition is written generically in `l`; finding a `Z(π/2^l)`-to-any-`D_l`
magic-state factory at a level or parameter range not yet swept only requires widening the sweep
(see `searches/README.md`).
